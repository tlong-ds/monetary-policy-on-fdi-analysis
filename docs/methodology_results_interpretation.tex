\chapter{Methodology and Results Interpretation}
\label{chap:methodology-results-interpretation}

\section{Data and Empirical Strategy}
\label{sec:data-empirical-strategy}

We study the association between domestic monetary conditions and FDI net inflows in an unbalanced panel of 11 ASEAN-oriented economies (Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Lao PDR, Malaysia, Myanmar, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Timor-Leste, Viet Nam) over 2000--2023 (max 264 country-year observations). FDI net inflows (\% of GDP) is the dependent variable. The liquidity channel is proxied by broad money growth (annual \%). The cost-of-capital channel is proxied by three alternative interest rates: the nominal deposit rate, the real interest rate, and the lending rate. Controls include inflation (GDP deflator), trade openness (\% of GDP), log GDP per capita, and exchange-rate depreciation (log-difference of LCU/USD). All monetary proxies and FDI are used as observed; controls are interpolated or edge-filled under strict rules.

The preferred specification is a two-way fixed-effects (TWFE) model:
\begin{equation}
    FDI_{it} = \beta_1 BM_{it} + \beta_2 IR_{it}^{(k)} + \boldsymbol{\gamma}'\mathbf{X}_{it} + \alpha_i + \lambda_t + \varepsilon_{it},
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_i$ and $\lambda_t$ are country and year fixed effects. The Hausman test rejects random effects across all specifications. We report Driscoll--Kraay (1998) standard errors, which are robust to heteroskedasticity, serial correlation, and cross-sectional dependence, making them appropriate for small-$N$, moderate-$T$ macro panels.

\section{Main Results}
\label{sec:main-results}

Two key findings emerge. First, the liquidity proxy (broad money growth) is generally positive but statistically imprecise across all headline models (Panel A), ranging from 0.035 (M4, $p>0.10$) to 0.122 (M3, $p<0.05$). This pattern is directionally consistent with a liquidity channel but lacks precision. Second, the cost-of-capital proxies do not deliver a stable negative association: the deposit and lending rates are positive and imprecise, while the real interest rate is negative but also imprecise ($-0.323$, $p>0.10$). Within-$R^2$ values are modest ($\le 0.09$), indicating limited within-country explanatory power after absorbing fixed effects.

The extended models (Panel B) add tourism arrivals (M6a, M6b) or the human capital index (M7a, M7b) to the real-rate and deposit-rate baselines respectively. Monetary coefficients remain stable, confirming that the headline imprecision is not driven by omitted structural controls. However, multicollinearity rises sharply in these specifications (max VIF $>170$), reinforcing their appendix status.

\section{Robustness Checks}
\label{sec:robustness}

Common-sample re-estimation confirms that coefficient differences across monetary proxies are not artifacts of sample composition. Restricting M1 to the M2 support reduces the broad-money coefficient from 0.046 to 0.037, and the lagged M3 specification is stable to this restriction. The lending-rate coefficient in M5 is not robust to harmonization with the M2 sample. These checks are reported in the companion robustness table.

Drop-collinearity diagnostics show that the severe VIF in M7a (387.19) is driven by the joint inclusion of the human capital index and log GDP per capita; omitting either variable resolves the issue (max VIF $\le 16.80$). The human-capital coefficient itself is imprecise regardless of specification (e.g., M7b: 0.165, SE 3.260).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Three caveats shape interpretation. (1) Short panel and identification: With at most 24 annual observations per country before missingness, and as few as 145 post-missingness, year fixed effects absorb common shocks and limited within-country variation remains for monetary variables. Coefficients are conditional associations, not causal effects. (2) Unbalanced proxy coverage: Cambodia has zero observed interest-rate years; Lao PDR and Timor-Leste have partial coverage. Estimation samples vary across models, and results may partly reflect the specific country composition of each specification. (3) Diagnostic fragility: Within-$R^2$ values are near zero; the real-rate model (M4) shows marginal cross-sectional dependence ($p=0.054$); the extended models (M6--M7) have extreme multicollinearity. The most defensible conclusion is that monetary conditions show directionally plausible but statistically fragile associations with FDI inflows in this panel.
